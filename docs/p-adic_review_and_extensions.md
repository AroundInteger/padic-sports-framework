# P-adic Clustering: Review of the Idealised Model and Extensions to Sport and Public Health

**Working notes — April 2026 (revised: PEF reframe)**

> **Revision note (April 2026).** This version incorporates the Paired Efficiency Factor (PEF) framework from the companion methodology paper. The original abs/rel synthesis (the "UP1 environmental noise cancellation" framing) is preserved as a special case but is now embedded in the more general PEF taxonomy, which makes per-dimension feature decisions principled rather than heuristic and gives a quantitative replacement for the previously unjustified exponential weights.

---

## 1. Where the project currently stands

Across the rugby paper draft and the prior design notes, the project has crystallised around three components:

1. **An idealised generative model — "Hierarchical Dominion".** Sixteen teams arranged in four levels of four (powers of two), with deterministic between-level dominance and discrete within-level strategic choices encoded over distinct primes (philosophy at p=2, formation at p=3, resources at p=5). Distance is taken as the max p-adic norm across the strategic dimensions, so the ultrametric inequality d(A,C) ≤ max{d(A,B), d(B,C)} holds by construction. The whole point of the construction is that it *guarantees* a silhouette score above 0.95 — it is a proof-of-concept that p-adic clustering can perfectly recover hierarchy when the data really is hierarchical.

2. **PEF (Paired Efficiency Factor) as the formal underpinning for abs vs. rel feature construction.** What was originally framed as an "abs/rel synthesis" with the verbal "UP1 environmental noise cancellation" intuition is in fact a special case of a more general result. PEF generalises Fisher's classical paired-efficiency formula 1/(1−ρ) to unequal variances via η = (1+κ)/(1+κ − 2√κ ρ), where κ = σ²_B / σ²_A is the variance ratio and ρ is the within-pair correlation. This gives a quantitative, distribution-free criterion for whether to use a relative feature (X_A − X_B) or to keep absolute features (X_A, X_B): η > 1 means pairing reduces variance and rel is preferred; η = 1 is the Fisher baseline; η < 1 means pairing amplifies variance, in which case rel may *still* be preferred if the information content I(X; Y) under a Gaussian discriminant model justifies it (the "efficiency–power tension"). Sport KPIs span all four quadrants of the (κ, ρ) plane: KPIs with positive ρ from shared environment (e.g. kicks-from-hand, carries) sit in Q1/Q2 with η > 1 — this is exactly the UP1 noise-cancellation case; KPIs with negative ρ from competitive dynamics (e.g. turnovers won, penalties conceded) sit in Q3/Q4 with η ≤ 1, and rugby's most non-trivial result is that rel still beats abs in Q4. Concrete consequences for the 7-dimensional vector: (a) abs vs. rel for each dimension becomes a quadrant-conditional decision computed from the data, not a fixed design choice; (b) the exponential weights (10 000 / 100 / 50 / 25 / 12 / 6 / 3) are replaced by per-dimension information content I(X_k; Y), which keeps the desired lexicographic structure but for a principled reason; (c) the empirical PEF-to-ML mapping ΔML = 0.234(η − 1) + 0.089(η − 1)² gives per-dimension predicted contributions to separability before any clustering is run.

3. **A real-data validation.** 1,128 matches across 16 URC/Pro14-era rugby teams over four seasons. The enhanced pipeline reaches a silhouette of 0.7083 at p=2, k=6 — a 126.7% improvement over the abs-only baseline (0.3125) and roughly 73% of the theoretical perfect-data ceiling (0.9746). The recovered clusters are tactically interpretable (Leinster as solo elite; Ulster/Bulls as forward-dominant kicking; Stormers/Edinburgh/Glasgow balanced-forward kicking; Munster less disciplined; Dragons/Zebre developing+undisciplined; a 7-team mid-table competitive disciplined cluster). Companion PEF analysis (URC, n=240 games) reports mean η = 1.845 across 25 KPI studies, with KPIs distributed across all four (κ, ρ) quadrants — so the rugby clustering result is, properly understood, a worked Q4 demonstration of PEF where rel features beat abs even though several individual KPIs have η < 1.

So the spine of a defensible paper is in place. Before extending, it's worth being explicit about what is *not* yet nailed down — these are the holes the extensions can be structured to fill.

### 1.1 Gaps in the current draft

**a. The "126.7% improvement" sits on a single baseline.** Comparison is to abs-only with the same pipeline, not to non-p-adic alternatives at parity. To convince a reviewer, the comparison set should include Ward's hierarchical clustering (Euclidean), k-means, and Gaussian mixtures on the same 7-d feature vector, plus p-adic on a *non-categorised* version of the same data, so we can attribute gains separately to (i) categorisation, (ii) ultrametric distance, and (iii) abs/rel synthesis. Murtagh and Contreras's Baire-metric work and the SIAM linear-time ultrametric embedding paper give us natural reference baselines.

**b. The exponential weights now have a principled replacement under PEF.** The weights (10 000 → 3) do the right qualitative job but were originally unprincipled. The PEF framework supplies the natural fix: weight each dimension k by its information content I(X_k; Y) under the Gaussian discriminant model, computed from per-dimension (κ̂_k, ρ̂_k, δ̂_k) via PEF equation eq:mi_closed. This preserves the lexicographic property the original weights wanted (high-information dimensions dominate the encoding) while making the weight schedule reproducible from the data. Cross-validation across the prime sweep then becomes meaningful, because each weight is anchored in a measurable quantity rather than chosen.

**c. "Optimal prime p=2" needs a sensitivity analysis.** Rugby has natural binary splits at most strategic dimensions (kick / don't kick, forward / back oriented), so p=2 winning is plausible — but a sweep across p ∈ {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13} on silhouette and Davies–Bouldin, with a story about *why* the optimum lands where it does, is the more publishable form of the result. The current draft mentions "p = 11, 13" only in future work.

**d. Cluster stability is unvalidated.** Silhouette is one metric; bootstrap resampling, Jaccard cluster stability, and cross-season validation (do the same teams cluster together year over year?) would substantially strengthen the empirical claim. The 1,128 matches naturally support a leave-one-season-out CV.

**e. The idealised-vs-real story isn't quantified end-to-end.** The "progressive approximation" idea from the design notes (Level 0 perfect → Level 4 real, with predicted silhouette decay 0.95 → 0.55+) is a powerful narrative but isn't currently shown empirically as a curve. Doing this by *injecting* increasing amounts of noise into Hierarchical Dominion data and watching silhouette degrade would let you map real rugby onto a point on that curve.

**f. No outcome validation.** Cluster membership has not (yet) been tested as a predictor of anything external — match outcomes, league position the next season, derby-style fixture surprises. That's the cleanest way to show the clustering captures something *real*, not just internally consistent geometry. The PEF-to-ML mapping ΔML = 0.234(η − 1) + 0.089(η − 1)² also provides a *predicted* per-dimension contribution to ML accuracy that can be validated dimension by dimension before the clustering is even run.

**g. Per-dimension quadrant placement of the seven constructed dimensions.** The PEF paper places 25 raw KPIs in the (κ, ρ) plane; the rugby p-adic pipeline operates on seven *constructed* dimensions (e.g. abs_carries+passes for attacking style, abs_scrums+lineouts for set piece). Summation changes (κ, ρ) — Var(X+Y) = σ²_X + σ²_Y + 2ρ_XY σ_X σ_Y — so the constructed dimension's PEF is not the average of its constituents and must be computed directly. The natural next experiment is to place each of the seven constructed dimensions individually in the (κ, ρ) plane, report their η values, and inspect quadrant heterogeneity. Expectations: performance tier and breakdown mastery sit deep in Q1 (high η); territory control and set piece sit closer to Q2 (positive ρ, lower κ); discipline and penetration likely span Q3/Q4. If that prediction holds, the abs/rel choice in the current pipeline is approximately right by accident — but explicit PEF placement makes the result defensible.

These gaps are not show-stoppers — they are the next round of work. I'll thread them into the extensions because the same structure (idealised model → bridge → real data → external validation) repeats cleanly in the new domains.

## 2. What the literature actually contains

Three observations from the search are worth foregrounding because they shape the positioning, plus a fourth that follows from the in-house PEF methodology paper.

**Observation 1: there is a small, mature literature on p-adic / ultrametric clustering as a *general method*, but virtually none of it is applied to sport.** The methodological backbone goes back to Fionn Murtagh's programme — ultrametric topology, Baire-metric clustering with linear-time complexity, the explicit linkage between p-adic encodings and hierarchical classification ([Murtagh 2011 SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/060676532); [Murtagh & Contreras 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S2070046612010062); [Murtagh "Thinking ultrametrically, thinking p-adically"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4939-0742-7_16)). More recent work continues to refine this — [Bradley 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/8/1284) on norm-based p-adic operations for robust information processing, and [Local Ultrametricity 2025 *J. Classification*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00357-025-09508-3) testing ultrametricity via p-adic Mumford curves. There is one direct domain analogue in commerce — [Nilaish 2024 SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5326162) on p-adic ultrametrics for customer segmentation — which is structurally identical to what the rugby paper is doing for teams, but in a different vertical. So the rugby paper is not just a sport contribution; it is plausibly the first applied p-adic clustering paper in sports analytics, which makes the methodological positioning easier than I'd expected.

**Observation 2: the talent ID and tournament-design literatures are crying out for hierarchy-respecting methods, even if they don't know it yet.** The talent identification systematic reviews ([racket sports systematic review 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10772031/); [thirty-year longitudinal review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1750984X.2024.2309623); [team-sports ML systematic review 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12912669/)) all converge on the same complaint: athlete data is *intrinsically* multidimensional and longitudinal, and current methods either use Euclidean clustering that treats all dimensions as commensurable, or supervised ML that requires a label and so cannot detect novel pathway types. Talent pathways are by their structure tree-like — junior → academy → development squad → senior squad → international — and the [Kovalchik-style talent-transfer paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11571061/) shows that clustering sports themselves by task/individual/environment similarities yields useful transfer recommendations. Tournament design has its own rich body of work on fairness axioms ([Schwenk's three axioms](http://www.oxfordcroquet.com/tech/knockout3/What_is_the_Correct_Way_to_Seed_a_Knockout_Tournam.pdf); [Karpov's "theory of knockout tournament seedings"](https://www.uni-heidelberg.de/md/awi/forschung/dp600.pdf); [Toukmaji 2022 chalk index](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/cmsac/conference/2022/assets/pdf/ToukmajiChristopher.pdf)) and on the algebra of bracket placement (i + j = 2^r + 1) — but the connection between bracket structure and ultrametric distance is, to my reading, never made explicit in that literature.

**Observation 3: in public health, the p-adic / ultrametric *epidemic* literature already exists, the p-adic *clinical* literature does not.** Andrei Khrennikov's COVID-era series ([2021 *Physica A*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8294751/); [2020 ultrametric SIR random walk](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7597204/); [medRxiv preprint on Sweden herd immunity](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.07.15.20154419v1.full)) is by now the clear reference point: he models the population as a tree of social clusters, virus carriers as random walkers on a p-adic energy landscape, transmission as jumping between ultrametric balls separated by social barriers, and shows the model produces a power-law approach to herd immunity rather than the exponential decay of standard SIR. That is a worked example of the same mathematical core the rugby paper uses, applied to disease spread. By contrast, the multimorbidity clustering literature ([2024 Nature Communications systematic review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67372-6); [2024 *Communications Medicine* multi-resolution clusters in 10M primary-care patients](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-024-00529-4); [2025 European Geriatric Medicine actionable interventions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41999-025-01270-4)) and the patient-stratification literature ([2024 Springer EHR clustering comparison](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44196-024-00568-8); [Nature *npj Digital Medicine* 2020 deep stratification](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-020-0301-z); [PMC 2024 explainable hierarchical clustering for subtyping](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10854470/); [phenotype clustering narrative review 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9411746/)) overwhelmingly use Ward's-with-Euclidean, k-means, GMM, latent class analysis, network methods, and increasingly deep representations — ultrametric or p-adic methods are absent. This is striking because clinical hierarchies are deeply nested (system → trust → hospital → ward → clinical team → patient → episode), exactly the structure Khrennikov exploits epidemiologically.

**Observation 4 (in-house): the PEF paper supplies the missing methodological backbone for abs vs. rel feature construction across all of these domains.** The Paired Efficiency Factor result generalises Fisher's classical paired-efficiency formula to unequal variances, derives a closed-form relationship between η and the mutual information I(X; Y) under bivariate normality, and partitions the (κ, ρ) plane into four quadrants with distinct feature-engineering implications. Rugby (mean η = 1.845, n = 240 games) and football (mean η = 1.623, n = 552 games) are the primary worked examples; cross-domain validation in healthcare (NHANES blood-pressure pairings, η = 2.533, Q2), genomics (GSE47462, η = 2.70), finance (S&P 500 pairings, η = 3.129, Q1), and manufacturing (Bosch CNC, η = 1.382) shows the framework generalises. The relevance to the p-adic clustering programme is that PEF tells us *which* of the seven constructed dimensions in the rugby pipeline should use rel vs. abs, *how heavily* each should be weighted in the base-p encoding, and what the predicted ML lift per dimension is. The same logic transfers wholesale to the public-health extensions in §4: NHS trust pairs within a region sit in Q1/Q2 (high ρ from shared regional shocks); LSOA pairs within a local authority similarly sit in Q1/Q2 on WIMD domains; cross-region or cross-LA pairs drift toward Q3/Q4 and need the I(X; Y) check.

The cleanest framing for the paper, then, is that p-adic clustering is the natural mathematical formalism whenever:

- the underlying structure is genuinely tree-like (organisational, taxonomic, phylogenetic, hierarchical-by-construction);
- categorisation of continuous variables is defensible (because the data has natural thresholds, or because a stakeholder cares about discrete tiers);
- relative features are appropriate, with PEF providing the per-dimension (κ̂, ρ̂) → η decision rule and information-content weighting; and
- you want a clustering that *respects* the hierarchy rather than treating it as a coincidence of the Euclidean geometry.

Sport tournaments and clinical hierarchies are paradigm cases. The rugby paper is the proof-of-concept (and a worked Q4 demonstration of PEF); the extensions in §3 and §4 are the natural follow-ons.

## 3. Extension 1 — Sport: talent pathways and tournament structure

### 3.1 Talent pathways and athlete development

Athlete development is *by construction* a tree. A 12-year-old in a regional age-group squad either does or does not progress to the academy. From the academy they either do or do not earn a senior contract. From the senior contract they either do or do not earn international selection. The state space at each branching point is small, the transitions are temporally ordered, and athletes who diverge early into different branches have ultrametrically large distances regardless of how similar their physical metrics are now — exactly the property that motivated p-adic distance in Hierarchical Dominion.

**Idealised model: "Pathway Tree".** Define a perfect athlete-development system with depth d=4 (regional → academy → senior → international) and branching factor b=4 at each level, producing 256 terminal "pathway addresses". Each athlete is encoded as a base-b digit string (a₁, a₂, a₃, a₄) where aᵢ ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3} is the choice at level i. Take p=b=4 (or p=2 if the branchings are binary "selected / not selected" with sub-decisions). The p-adic norm of the difference between two athletes' addresses is then exactly p^(-k) where k is the depth of their lowest common ancestor — pathway distance is *literally* tree distance, by construction.

**Bridge to real data.** Real talent data is messy in three specific ways the framework can absorb:

1. *Multidimensional features within each pathway level.* At any given level, an athlete has continuous physical, technical, psychological, and tactical scores — exactly the multidimensional longitudinal panel emphasised in the [racket-sports systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10772031/) and the [thirty-year longitudinal meta-aggregation](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1750984X.2024.2309623). Treat each level as its own feature vector and use the abs/rel synthesis: abs metrics (e.g. abs sprint time, abs VO₂max) capture intrinsic capability, rel metrics (e.g. position relative to age-group mean) capture competitive standing. Categorise within each level using natural thresholds (national norms, percentile bands), then encode level-by-level as a base-p digit string.
2. *Drop-outs and re-entries.* Use a special digit (e.g. p−1) to encode "not progressed at this level" and another (e.g. 0) for "skipped level / fast-tracked". The ultrametric topology naturally separates pathways with different progression histories — two athletes who both drop out at the academy stage are close, regardless of where they re-enter.
3. *Sport-of-origin transfers.* The [Kovalchik-style talent-transfer paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11571061/) clusters 34 sports by task/individual/environmental similarity. That sport-similarity tree can be the *highest-significance* dimension in the encoding (above the within-sport pathway), so cross-sport transferees end up close to within-sport athletes whose pathways most resemble theirs.

**What this lets us answer.** With a longitudinal cohort of ~5–10 years of academy-and-above data from a single pathway (rugby academies are a natural target given the data already in hand), we could ask: do successful international athletes share a pathway *cluster* signature, or are they spread across pathway clusters? Are there "late-bloomer" pathway clusters that current selection processes systematically miss? Does the pathway cluster of a 16-year-old predict their senior career better than their current physical metrics alone? These are exactly the questions the [team-sports ML systematic review 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12912669/) flags as the open methodological frontier.

**Validation design.** Use the same idealised → bridged → real progression as the rugby paper. Generate a Pathway Tree synthetic cohort (perfect tree, ≥0.95 silhouette). Inject increasing noise (random progression flips, late selections, missing levels) and watch silhouette degrade — that gives the prediction curve onto which real data lands. External validation: predict senior caps or salary at age 25 from pathway cluster at age 18, against a null model of physical metrics alone.

**PEF placement of athlete-development features.** The (κ, ρ) structure of talent metrics is itself level-dependent. Within an age-cohort academy, paired physical metrics (sprint vs. sprint, jump vs. jump for cohort members measured in the same testing block) sit in Q1/Q2 — high ρ from shared testing conditions, modest κ from broadly comparable athlete variability — so relativisation against cohort mean works straightforwardly with η > 1. Across academies, where competitive selection induces negative ρ (the best academy hoards top-quartile athletes and starves the next), the same metric drifts toward Q4 and the relativisation decision needs the I(X; Y) check from PEF eq:mi_closed. This dovetails with the pathway-tree encoding: the highest-significance digit (region/academy) sits in Q4 territory; the lowest-significance digits (within-cohort dimensions) sit in Q1/Q2. Information-content weighting then gives a principled basis for the exponential weight schedule.

### 3.2 Tournament structure and seeding

A knockout bracket is the textbook ultrametric object. Two competitors who can only meet in the final are at maximal bracket distance; two who meet in round one are at minimal bracket distance; the "round at which they could meet" is exactly the height of their lowest common ancestor in the bracket tree, and the standard placement rule i + j = 2^r + 1 is a statement about p=2-adic addresses on the bracket. The connection has not, to my reading, been made explicitly in the tournament-design literature ([Schwenk's axioms](http://www.oxfordcroquet.com/tech/knockout3/What_is_the_Correct_Way_to_Seed_a_Knockout_Tournam.pdf); [Karpov's monograph](https://www.uni-heidelberg.de/md/awi/forschung/dp600.pdf); [Toukmaji 2022](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/cmsac/conference/2022/assets/pdf/ToukmajiChristopher.pdf); [arXiv 2508.19848 hierarchy in pairwise contests](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19848)) — that is the methodological gap.

**Idealised model: "Perfect Bracket".** A 2^n-team single-elimination bracket where each competitor i is assigned a binary address aᵢ ∈ {0,1}^n. The 2-adic distance |aᵢ − aⱼ|₂ is exactly 2^(−k) where k is the round at which i and j first *could* meet. Skill is a real number sᵢ; outcomes are deterministic when |sᵢ − sⱼ| is large and random when small (a Bradley–Terry kernel works). The "Schwenk axioms" of delayed confrontation, sincerity rewarded, and favouritism minimised translate into 2-adic statements about the assignment of seeds to addresses.

**Three concrete contributions this enables.**

1. *A p-adic seeding fairness metric.* Define unfairness as the 2-adic correlation between seed and address — a fair seeding distributes top seeds across the bracket so that no two top-k seeds share a low-order address bit. Schwenk's standard zoning method is one specific solution; equal-gap and increasing-competitive-intensity alternatives are others. Re-deriving them as p-adic optimisation problems gives a unified framework and cleanly defines new criteria (e.g. "minimum total p-adic seed correlation" as an aggregate fairness score).
2. *Upset detection via p-adic anomaly.* An "upset" is structurally an event where a low-seed competitor reaches a round their address shouldn't have. Define an upset score for a tournament as the average 2-adic distance between expected-finalist addresses and actual-finalist addresses. This generalises "chalk vs. surprise" measures (the [Toukmaji chalk index](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/cmsac/conference/2022/assets/pdf/ToukmajiChristopher.pdf)) into a hierarchy-respecting score that distinguishes round-1 surprises from semi-final surprises automatically.
3. *Bracket clustering for cross-tournament comparison.* Cluster historical tournaments (March Madness years, Wimbledon years, World Cups) by the p-adic distance between their upset-score profiles. Years with similar bracket-shape outcomes (chalky early rounds + chaotic semis vs. uniform chaos vs. dominant top-seed runs) end up close. This is a hypothesis-generating tool for "what kind of competition was this?".

**Real-data validation.** ATP / WTA Slams, NCAA brackets, the Rugby World Cup, FIFA World Cup knockouts — all publicly available, all naturally bracketed, all decades of history. Idealised-model baseline is "rank-perfect bracket where seed 1 always beats seed 2" (silhouette 1.0 by construction); real-data upper bound is what historical tournaments actually achieve. The same progressive-approximation curve from rugby should reappear.

**PEF connection.** Pairwise match outcomes within a tournament are exactly the sport setting PEF was developed for, and bracket placement *induces* a structured set of pairings whose (κ, ρ) profile evolves through the rounds. Round-1 pairs are deliberately chosen to be high-asymmetry (top seed vs. low seed: large κ, expected positive ρ from shared event conditions, deep Q1) so η is large and the predicted upset rate is low; quarter-finals onward narrow toward κ ≈ 1 with potentially negative ρ from intense competitive dynamics, drifting toward Q4. Predicted versus observed upset rates per round therefore become a direct test of PEF on a structured dataset, and historical bracket clusters can be profiled by their round-by-round (κ, ρ) trajectory rather than by aggregate chalk-vs-surprise scores.

## 4. Extension 2 — Public health: healthcare delivery hierarchies and population risk stratification

The shift from sport to public health is shorter than it looks. Both have ordered hierarchies, both have abs/rel structure (a hospital's intrinsic mortality rate vs. its rate relative to peers), both already have rich literatures on hierarchical *modelling* (multilevel regression) but thin literatures on hierarchical *clustering* with the right metric.

### 4.1 Healthcare delivery hierarchies

The English NHS (and most analogous public systems) is canonically nested: Region → Integrated Care System → NHS Trust → Hospital → Department → Ward → Clinical team → Patient → Episode. Multilevel modelling explicitly recognises this — patients within wards within hospitals within trusts is the textbook example in the [NCBI Multilevel Data Structures chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK565716/). Multilevel models partition variance across levels; what they don't do is *cluster providers* respecting the hierarchy.

**Idealised model: "Perfect Provider Tree".** A system with d=5 levels (region, ICS, trust, hospital, ward), branching b=4 at each level → 1024 terminal wards. Each ward gets a base-b address. Performance metrics at each level are encoded as digits in a base-p number, with the most significant digit being region-level performance and the least significant being ward-level case-mix-adjusted outcome. The p-adic distance between two wards is dominated by the highest level at which they differ — wards in different regions are p-adically far regardless of how similar their throughput numbers happen to be.

**PEF maps cleanly onto NHS performance metrics, with quadrant-aware abs vs. rel decisions.** Absolute metrics — bed count, staff FTE, intrinsic capability (what can this trust do?). Relative metrics — standardised mortality ratio, readmission rate vs. peer group, four-hour A&E performance vs. national benchmark (how is this trust doing relative to the rest of the system?). Under PEF, the choice between abs and rel is set by the (κ, ρ) of the relevant pairing: trust pairs *within* the same region or ICS sit in Q1/Q2 with high positive ρ from shared regional shocks (winter pressures, regulatory cycles, local population flow) and modest κ — relativisation here is the unambiguous winner with η well above 1, and the noise-cancellation reading of UP1 is precisely correct in this regime. A flu surge lifts admissions across all trusts in a region; subtracting the regional mean removes the common shock cleanly. Trust pairs *across* regions sit closer to Q3 or Q4 — ρ falls because regional shocks no longer co-vary, and κ rises because specialist trusts and district generals have visibly different variance structures. In Q4 the abs vs. rel choice needs the explicit I(X; Y) check; rel does not automatically win, and naive cross-region benchmarking can be statistically harmful. This is exactly the heterogeneity that breaks Euclidean clustering on national peer groups, and that hierarchical p-adic encoding (region as highest-significance digit, ward as lowest) fixes — but now with PEF giving the *quantitative* basis for why region-level pairing is the right primary level.

**Three useful applications.**

1. *Hierarchy-respecting peer comparison.* Today, NHS Digital benchmarking groups hospitals into peer groups via a few categorical filters (teaching, specialist, district general). A p-adic clustering would generate peer groups *from* the data while respecting the regional/system hierarchy — useful for fairer benchmarking of providers in atypical regions.
2. *Quality-improvement target identification.* A hospital that clusters with peers in its region but appears as a relative outlier on, say, breakdown-discipline analogues (sepsis recognition, falls prevention) becomes a clear improvement target *because* the clustering controls for regional context. The current dominance of [Ward's-Euclidean methods in EHR-based phenotyping](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44196-024-00568-8) cannot do this cleanly because Euclidean distance does not honour the regional partition.
3. *System-restructuring impact analysis.* When ICSs were created in 2022, they partly reorganised the regional hierarchy. A pre/post p-adic clustering of trusts would show whether the new hierarchical encoding actually changed *cluster membership* (i.e. whether the structural change had statistical reality) or whether it was bureaucratic relabelling.

**Pilot dataset.** NHS Digital publishes Hospital Episode Statistics and the System Oversight Framework metrics at trust level annually. A first pilot needs only ~150 trusts × ~30 metrics × ~5 years to replicate the rugby pipeline structure (1,128 matches × 16 teams × 4 seasons is comparable scale).

### 4.2 Population health and risk stratification

The multimorbidity clustering literature is the most direct analogue. The [2024 Nature Communications meta-analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67372-6) catalogues multimorbidity clusters across populations; the [2024 *Communications Medicine* multi-resolution paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-024-00529-4) clusters 10 million English primary-care patients at multiple resolutions; the [2025 European Geriatric Medicine review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41999-025-01270-4) translates this into actionable interventions. Method-wise this literature uses hierarchical clustering (Ward's), latent class analysis, k-centre, network analysis, and increasingly deep representations ([npj Digital Medicine 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-020-0301-z)). The opportunity is to bring p-adic encoding to a setting where the hierarchies are *demographic and geographic* even before they are clinical: patients within households within LSOAs within local authorities within regions, simultaneously stratified by deprivation deciles within nation-level distributions.

**Idealised model: "Hierarchical Population".** Treat the population as a tree where the levels are geography (region → local authority → LSOA), demography (age band, sex), and clinical (multimorbidity profile). Each person gets a multi-prime encoding: geography in p=2 (or higher branching for fine geography), demographic strata in p=3, clinical phenotype digits in p=5. The patient's full address is a base-(2,3,5)-mixed p-adic number. Distance respects the hierarchy: two patients in different regions are far regardless of demographic match; among patients in the same region, those in different deprivation deciles are far regardless of clinical similarity; within deprivation, multimorbidity drives differentiation.

**Why this is non-trivially better than the current literature, with PEF making the abs/rel decision quadrant-aware.** The Welsh Index of Multiple Deprivation work the project already touched (1,909 LSOAs, the [WIMD pipeline notes](https://gov.wales/welsh-index-multiple-deprivation)) is exactly the kind of hierarchical geographic dataset where Euclidean clustering loses. Two LSOAs with identical income and employment scores but in different regions of Wales are *not* operationally interchangeable — they sit under different health boards, different deprivation context, different policy reach — and PEF makes precise the conditions under which relativisation captures that vs. when it does not. WIMD income and employment scores are strongly positively correlated *within* a local authority (people in adjacent LSOAs tend to share economic conditions): Q1 territory, η ≫ 1, rel ranks are unambiguously better than absolute scores for clustering. *Across* local authorities (and especially across rural / urban or coastal / inland boundaries), correlations weaken or flip, drifting toward Q3/Q4 — the abs/rel decision now needs the I(X; Y) check, and absolute scores may carry more information than ranks. The natural construction is a level-conditional encoding: rel within local authority (highest-information digits), abs across local authority (where rel may be statistically harmful), with weights set by per-domain I(X; Y).

**Three applications.**

1. *Risk stratification with stable cluster membership.* Current LACE / Charlson / Cambridge-multimorbidity scores are individual-level continuous risk scores; clusters are post-hoc. A p-adic risk stratification produces clusters first, with risk scores within cluster — and because the metric respects geography-and-deprivation, the same patient does not flip clusters when moving from urban to rural areas with similar absolute deprivation.
2. *Equity-aware intervention targeting.* If you cluster respecting deprivation, you avoid the trap of identifying a "high-risk" cluster that turns out to be an artefact of geography or socioeconomics rather than clinical need. The relative-metric noise cancellation is the formal version of "compared to peers in the same socioeconomic context, this cluster is unusually high-risk".
3. *Outbreak and surveillance early signal.* Khrennikov's [ultrametric diffusion model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8294751/) applies to this stratification *directly*: if your population is p-adically encoded by social/geographic structure, his treelike diffusion equation models the propagation of a disease across cluster boundaries with a parameter that controls between-level "social barriers". PEF gives a tractable empirical handle on this parameter: between-cluster KPI pairs (case counts, contact frequencies, syndromic signal levels for adjacent LSOAs or ICSs) have a measurable (κ̂, ρ̂), and Khrennikov's social-barrier height is directly proportional to the strength of decoupling — i.e. to (1 − ρ) for the matched cross-cluster pair. So the diffusion parameter is no longer a fitted heuristic; it can be estimated from the same paired data PEF already analyses. This connects population stratification to epidemic surveillance in one mathematical framework, and gives the Khrennikov bridge a quantitative form the current public-health literature has not articulated.

**Validation strategy.** The progressive-approximation pattern translates directly: synthetic Hierarchical Population data → noise injection → real data from a cohort like CPRD Aurum (~13M English primary-care patients) or SAIL (Welsh population data with WIMD linkage). External validation: predict 12-month emergency admission, mortality, or COVID outcomes from cluster membership, against a null model of LACE alone.

## 5. A unifying synthesis

What sport tournaments, talent pathways, hospital systems, and population health share is not "hierarchy" in a loose narrative sense — it is the specific mathematical property that the *meaningful* distance between entities is dominated by the highest level at which they differ. Two academy players in different sports are far apart no matter how similar their sprint times. Two trusts in different regions are far apart no matter how similar their bed numbers. Two LSOAs in different deprivation deciles are far apart no matter how similar their employment scores. This is *exactly* the ultrametric inequality d(A,C) ≤ max{d(A,B), d(B,C)}, which is *exactly* the defining property of the p-adic norm.

PEF does the orthogonal work of deciding, per dimension, whether the relevant signal lives in the absolute measurement or in the difference of paired measurements — and at what weight, via the information content under a Gaussian discriminant model. The abs/rel synthesis the project originally articulated is the verbal Q1/Q2 case of PEF (positive ρ, η > 1, rel wins through noise cancellation); the more interesting Q4 case (negative ρ, η < 1, rel still wins on information-content grounds) gives the rugby pipeline its non-trivial result. Categorisation with information-content weighting puts the per-dimension PEF decisions into a fixed-precision base-p encoding so that ordinal levels never get drowned out by within-level wobble. Clustering on the resulting p-adic distance respects the hierarchy by construction.

The methodological payoff is practical: better separated clusters (the rugby silhouette result), interpretable cluster boundaries (each cluster is a base-p digit pattern, readable as a strategic profile), and external-validation hooks (predict outcomes from cluster membership). The publication payoff is positional: the rugby paper plus these extensions together stake out p-adic clustering as a generalist hierarchy-respecting method, with worked applications in two underserved domains (talent ID and clinical hierarchies).

### 5.1 Suggested next steps — health-led ordering

The roadmap is reorganised around data accessibility. NHS System Oversight Framework metrics, WIMD, NHANES, and the GEO genomics validation set are all publicly available; URC academy data for the talent-pathway pilot sits behind partnership agreements. The near-term track therefore prioritises work that is data-ready and policy-resonant; the sports talent-pathway pilot proceeds in parallel as a separate study when academy access permits.

**Near-term track (entirely data-ready):**

1. *Tighten the rugby paper with PEF integration.* Add the baseline comparisons (Ward's, k-means, GMM at parity), the prime sweep p ∈ {2,3,5,7,11,13}, the bootstrap stability check, the leave-one-season-out CV, and the noise-injection curve onto which real data lands. *Additionally*: place each of the seven constructed dimensions individually in the (κ, ρ) plane, report η and I(X; Y) per dimension, and replace the exponential weights (10 000 → 3) with information-content weights derived from PEF eq:mi_closed. This closes gaps (a)–(g) of §1.1 and reframes the empirical contribution as a worked Q4 demonstration of PEF.

2. *Write the PEF / p-adic methodological note.* ~2,500 words; companion to the main paper. Explicitly bridges PEF and p-adic clustering: how per-dimension (κ̂, ρ̂) → η decisions feed into the abs/rel choice, how I(X; Y) sets the digit weights in the base-p encoding, and how the PEF-to-ML mapping ΔML = 0.234(η−1) + 0.089(η−1)² translates into expected silhouette gains. The small but novel theoretical contribution is the derivation of expected silhouette under p-adic distance as a function of per-dimension I(X; Y) — unaddressed in the PEF paper and closing the loop between PEF's classification metric and p-adic clustering's silhouette metric. Crucially, no new data is required, so this can be drafted in parallel with step 1.

3. *Pilot the NHS-trust extension* on publicly released System Oversight Framework data. ~150 trusts × ~30 metrics × ~5 years. Compute PEF for within-region and across-region trust pairings to confirm the predicted Q1/Q2 → Q3/Q4 drift, then use the result to set abs/rel per metric in the p-adic encoding. The output is a set of trust clusters that can be validated against existing peer groups and used to flag outliers — direct policy interest, with a quantitatively defensible methodology. Aim for *BMJ Quality & Safety* or *Health Services Research*.

4. *Pilot the WIMD-led population-health extension* on the publicly available Welsh Index of Multiple Deprivation (1,909 LSOAs). Aim for *Lancet Public Health* (regional) or *Health & Place* (methodological).

5. *Bridge the Khrennikov connection through PEF.* The theoretical synthesis that ties p-adic clustering, PEF, and ultrametric epidemic modelling together. By this stage, the NHS-trust and WIMD pilots have produced empirical (κ̂, ρ̂) estimates for cross-cluster KPI pairs, which supply the social-barrier-height parameter directly — the bridge paper effectively writes itself once steps 3–4 are complete. This is likely the most publishable single contribution across the project.
